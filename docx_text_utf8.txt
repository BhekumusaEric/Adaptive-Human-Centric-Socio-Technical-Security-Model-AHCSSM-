Introduction Social engineering (SE) represents one of the most significant cybersecurity threats because it exploits human psychology rather than technological vulnerabilities [ 1 ], [ 2 ] . Techniques such as phishing, vishing, and pretexting manipulate individuals into disclosing sensitive information or performing actions that compromise security systems. Recent cybersecurity reports show that a large proportion of cyber incidents involve a human element, demonstrating that users remain a critical vulnerability within organizational security environments [ 4 ], [ 5 ] . As attackers increasingly employ artificial intelligence tools to generate convincing fraudulent communications, traditional technical defenses alone are insufficient [ 3 ] . To address this challenge, researchers have proposed socio technical mitigation models that combine technological defenses with human centered security strategies [11], [12] . However, existing research remains fragmented and lacks a consolidated understanding of effective mitigation approaches. This study therefore conducts a systematic literature review to analyze existing socio technical mitigation models and identify key challenges, trends, and future research directions. This systematic review addresses this gap by examining the current research landscape on socio-technical models that mitigate human vulnerability to social engineering. The study is guided by three central research questions: What key challenges and observable trends influence the development and implementation of socio-technical models for reducing human susceptibility to social engineering? What types, components, strengths, and limitations characterize existing socio-technical models designed to protect individuals from such attacks? And finally, what future research directions emerge from the current body of literature on human vulnerability and socio-technical mitigation strategies? To address these questions, the review pursues two primary research objectives: to investigate and categorize the current and emerging challenges and trends associated with mitigating human susceptibility to social engineering, and to evaluate the strengths, limitations, and core components of existing socio-technical mitigation models reported in the literature. Together, these research questions and objectives provide a structured foundation for understanding the complexities of human-centered security within socio-technical contexts. 2 . Methodology This study employs a Systematic Literature Review ( SLR ) approach guided by the PRISMA framework [6] . The review process followed four stages: identification, screening, eligibility assessment, and final inclusion of relevant studies. A structured search strategy combining keywords related to social engineering, human vulnerability, and mitigation frameworks was applied across major academic databases including Scopus, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and the ACM Digital Library. Inclusion criteria required studies to be peer reviewed, written in English, and focused on mitigation models integrating human and technical security components. Studies focusing solely on technical detection mechanisms or attack classification were excluded. The final dataset consisted of eleven studies that met the predefined inclusion criteria. 2 .1 Study Selection Process The study selection process followed the four-stage procedure defined by the PRISMA framework, which includes identification, screening, eligibility assessment, and final inclusion of relevant studies. The objective of this process was to systematically filter the retrieved literature to ensure that only studies relevant to socio-technical mitigation models for social engineering were included in the final analysis [3], [12] . During the identification phase, the search strategy was applied across the selected academic databases, including Scopus, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and the ACM Digital Library. This initial search produced a set of potentially relevant records related to social engineering, human vulnerability, and mitigation frameworks. In the screening phase, duplicate records were removed , and the remaining studies were evaluated based on their titles and abstracts. Studies that did not focus on social engineering mitigation or that lacked a socio-technical perspective were excluded at this stage. The eligibility phase involved a full-text review of the remaining articles to determine whether they met the predefined inclusion criteria. Studies that focused solely on technical security mechanisms or that did not propose or evaluate a mitigation model were excluded. After applying all screening criteria, a final set of eleven studies was selected for inclusion in the systematic review. These studies form the basis of the descriptive and thematic analyses presented in the following sections. Discussion The findings of this study highlight several critical challenges in the design and implementation of socio-technical mitigation models for social engineering attacks. One major challenge is the lack of standardized frameworks that effectively integrate human, organizational, and technological components [3]. Many existing approaches address these components in isolation, limiting their overall effectiveness in real-world environments. Another key challenge is the difficulty of sustaining long-term behavioural change among users. While security awareness training programs can improve short-term awareness, their effectiveness often diminishes over time without continuous reinforcement [7]. This suggests that static training approaches are insufficient for addressing evolving social engineering threats. Furthermore, the increasing sophistication of social engineering attacks, particularly those enhanced by artificial intelligence, presents a significant challenge for traditional mitigation strategies. Attackers are now able to generate highly convincing phishing messages and impersonation attacks, making it more difficult for users to distinguish between legitimate and malicious communications [13], [14]. Despite these challenges, literature reveals several important trends. There is a clear shift toward adaptive and context-aware mitigation systems, which dynamically adjust security responses based on user behaviour and threat intelligence. Additionally, the integration of behavioural analytics and machine learning techniques is enabling more proactive detection of user vulnerabilities. The findings also indicate that effective mitigation requires a holistic socio-technical approach that combines behavioural insights, continuous training mechanisms, and technological controls. This reinforces the need for integrated frameworks that address the complexity of human-centered cybersecurity threats. Findings The analysis of the selected studies reveals several critical themes regarding the current landscape of social engineering mitigation, specifically concerning the nature of the problems faced and the solutions currently being deployed. 5 .1 Trending Socio-Technical Problems A significant trending problem identified in the literature is the persistence of human-centric vulnerabilities, where technical controls fail because attackers successfully exploit psychological triggers such as trust, urgency, and authority. Furthermore, the sustainability of training remains a major challenge , research indicates a decay in knowledge where users frequently revert to insecure behaviors over time despite completing security awareness programs. This issue is compounded by the presence of fragmented frameworks. Many current models are either overly focused on technical aspects or purely behavioral, failing to achieve a seamless integration where technology effectively supports human decision-making in real-time. 5 .2 Existing Socio-Technical Solutions In response to these challenges, several existing solutions have gained prominence, most notably gamified experiential learning. These systems move beyond static instruction by using simulations to allow users to experience and identify phishing attacks in a controlled environment. Additionally, researchers are increasingly incorporating behavioral modeling, utilizing psychological theories such as Protection Motivation Theory to predict and nudge safer user actions. More recently, the field has seen the emergence of adaptive socio-technical systems. These advanced models are designed to be context-aware, dynamically adjusting security protocols and interventions based on a userâ€™s immediate environment and observed vulnerability level. Future Research Directions The findings of this systematic literature review underscore the critical necessity of evolving from static security interventions toward more dynamic, human-centric defenses. While current research has established a foundational understanding of socio-technical mitigation, several avenues for future investigation remain. First, there is a clear requirement for longitudinal studies that assess the long-term sustainability of behavioral changes induced by gamified and experiential learning models. Current evidence suggests a "decay" in security awareness over time, and future research must address how to maintain high levels of vigilance without causing security fatigue. Furthermore, the integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning presents a significant opportunity to develop adaptive mitigation systems that can respond to real-time psychological triggers. Future studies should focus on the ethical implications and technical feasibility of context-aware models that monitor user behavior to provide just-in-time interventions. Additionally, research should expand to investigate the cross-cultural effectiveness of these models, ensuring that socio-technical defenses are robust across diverse organizational and regional contexts. By addressing these gaps, the academic community can move toward a unified framework that seamlessly integrates human behavioral insights with advanced technological safeguards. 7 . Conclusion This systematic literature review has synthesized the current state of socio-technical models designed to mitigate human vulnerability to social engineering. By analyzing eleven primary studies, the review identified a clear shift in the research landscape from traditional, one-size-fits-all training programs toward sophisticated, adaptive, and gamified interventions. The results emphasize that technical controls alone are insufficient to counter threats that exploit human psychology; rather, effective defense requires a holistic integration of behavioral insights and technological innovation. The thematic classification provided in this study offers a structured roadmap for researchers and practitioners to identify effective strategies, particularly highlighting the potential of AI-driven and experiential learning models. In conclusion, mitigating the risk of social engineering necessitates a paradigm shift where the human element is viewed not as the "weakest link," but as an empowered and supported component of a multi-layered security architecture. This study provides the consolidated evidence necessary to inform the development of more resilient, context-aware cybersecurity defenses in an increasingly complex threat environment . 8 .
